In empirical research, AI is often involved long before a text is written. It transcribes interviews, codes open-ended responses, writes analysis code, generates synthetic data or is itself trained as a model. It runs on one’s own computer, on university servers or with external providers. Anyone who wants to assess a result needs to know this. And for the people whose data were processed, it makes a difference where that happened.

[CLEAR](https://github.com/LeonieGantenbein/clear-kennzeichnung) labels how AI was involved in a text. PISCATOR labels how it was involved in the research on which a text is based. Eight short statements record how far the data refer to persons, where the AI ran, what it contributed to the code, who developed the research question and design, which models and tools were used, what the AI did with the data, where the data come from and who checked the AI’s results.

The model is the catch report. In a few fixed entries, it records what was caught, where, with what and when. It is brief and standardised, and that is precisely why it actually gets filled in. PISCATOR aims to be just as simple: a one-liner that fits into the methods section.

Such a statement does not claim to certify a result. Ludwik Fleck and Thomas S. Kuhn showed that scientific knowledge does not simply accumulate until everything has been uncovered. It emerges within thought styles and paradigms, with particular instruments and procedures. PISCATOR makes visible which of these procedures an AI helped to carry out. This helps others to place a finding, follow it and, where possible, replicate it.

Anyone publishing research gives both statements: PISCATOR for the research process, CLEAR for the text.

> **Two statements, one principle.** PISCATOR takes its logic, three letters and the suffixes (p), (n) and (t) from CLEAR: they mean the same in both. Anyone who knows CLEAR can read PISCATOR. And as with CLEAR, responsibility for a result always remains with whoever publishes it, not with the AI.

## Which research

PISCATOR is intended for empirical research that collects, prepares, codes or analyses data with code, whether quantitative or qualitative: interview studies and surveys, experiments and measurement series, corpus and archival work, modelling and simulation. Work without data processing of its own, such as theoretical, historical or hermeneutic studies, labels its text with CLEAR. A PISCATOR statement is not needed there.

A study often concerns only some of the statements. Whatever does not occur in a piece of research is marked with an X: SX means there is no code. This differs from S0, where there is code but it was written without AI. X is not a gap but a piece of information.

## Quick reference

The eight statements at a glance:

| Axis | Subject | Statement |
|---|---|---|
| **P** – Privacy | Personal reference of the data an AI processed | P0 no personal reference · P1 anonymised · P2 pseudonymised · P3 identifiable; suffix (s) for sensitive personal data; availability (p) published · (r) restricted · (e) planned · (n) not published |
| **I** – Infrastructure | Where the AI with access to the data ran | I0 local · I1 protected infrastructure of the institution · I2 external service with a data processing agreement · I3 external service with general terms of use only; suffix: country of server location, e.g. (CH) |
| **S** – Script | AI in programming | S0 without AI · S1 assistance · S2 parts generated · S3 largely generated · S4 fully generated; availability of code and prompts as for P |
| **C** – Conception | Research question, hypotheses, design, instruments | C0 exclusively human · C1 AI as a discussion partner · C2 individual parts worked out by the AI · C3 comprehensively generated by the AI |
| **A** – Attribution | Declaration of the tools | Models and tools with version, provider and type of use (applied, adapted, trained), programming languages, period |
| **T** – Transformation | What the AI did with the data | (co) collection · (ts) transcription · (oc) text recognition · (t) translation · (ps) pseudonymisation · (cl) cleaning · (im) imputation · (li) linkage · (ex) extraction · (su) summarisation · (an) coding · (mo) modelling; T0 without AI |
| **O** – Origin | Source and form of the data | O0 own collection only · O1 own and reused data · O2 reused data only; forms (or) original · (sc) scrambled · (ag) aggregated · (sy) synthetic · (si) simulated · (au) augmented |
| **R** – Responsibility | Checking of the AI’s results | R1 fully checked and taken responsibility for · R2 spot-checked · R3 unchecked; suffix (v) systematically validated |

```
PISCATOR (role): P(…)?, I(…)?, S(…)?, C?, A: models, languages, period, T(…), O(…)?, R(…)?
```

As in CLEAR, suffixes precede the number. For P, I, S, C and R, give the highest level reached; for T and the forms in O, list all applicable codes. If work steps differ considerably in personal reference or infrastructure, each step gets a line of its own.

**Where the label goes.**

- Article and book: in the methods section or the data availability statement
- Data and code repository: in the README
- Thesis: next to the declaration of authorship
- Data management plan and grant proposal: as planned use

The CLEAR line appears as usual at the end of the text or in the imprint.

**Two examples.** A sociologist conducts twenty interviews on living with a chronic illness, transcribes them with noScribe on her own computer, checks every transcript and codes by hand:

```
PISCATOR (author): P(s, n)3, I0, SX, C0, A: noScribe (Whisper large-v3, applied), 03–09/2026, T(ts), O(or)0, R1
```

A project team has a language model on the university server code the open-ended responses of its own survey, validates the coding on a sample and publishes a synthetic version of the data:

```
PISCATOR (project team): P(p, r)2, I1, S(p)2, C1, A: Llama 3.3 70B (applied), SDV CTGAN (trained on the survey data), Claude Code (Anthropic), Python 3.13, 02–07/2026, T(an), O(or, sy)0, R(v)2
```

All axes with explanations, guidance on classification and further examples, such as a model trained and applied in two steps, can be found in the chapter [“Specification”](SPECIFICATION.md). The [factsheet](FACTSHEET.en.md) summarises all abbreviations and contains a prompt with which a language model proposes a statement.

## Together with CLEAR

A publication carries both statements: PISCATOR in the methods section, CLEAR at the end. For the interview study, this would look as follows:

```
CLEAR (author): C0, L2, E(n)1, A: noScribe (Whisper large-v3), ChatGPT (GPT-5), 03–10/2026, R1
PISCATOR (author): P(s, n)3, I0, SX, C0, A: noScribe (Whisper large-v3, applied), 03–09/2026, T(ts), O(or)0, R1
```

CLEAR says how the text came about: the line of thought is the author’s own, and the AI smoothed the language and made her own unpublished material accessible. PISCATOR says in more detail what happened to the data. In CLEAR, C refers to the line of thought of the text; in PISCATOR, to the research question and design.

## Status

PISCATOR is a proposal. It builds on CLEAR but has not yet been tested in practice and is certainly still incomplete. Feedback from research, data management and ethics committees is welcome, preferably via the [issues in this repository](https://github.com/LeonieGantenbein/piscator-kennzeichnung/issues).

## About the name

Piscator is Latin for fisherman, hence the catch report as a model. The name also recalls the Fisherman’s Ring, the *Anulus Piscatoris*, with which papal documents were sealed until 1842. A seal shows who stands behind a document. In PISCATOR, as in CLEAR, this task falls to the statement R.
